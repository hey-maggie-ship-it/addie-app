Hi, I'm Maggie. I built Ankora, and this page is the story of why.

My career was built on turning strategy into execution: management consulting, standing up new capabilities at PE-backed companies and startups, scaling a DTC brand from $5M to $70M as its first executive hire, and most recently leading a $100M+ ecommerce division through a turnaround. Making plans happen was, quite literally, what I was paid to do.

Then life handed me a reset: my partner is active duty military, and we moved to Okinawa. I took it as the opening to chase a lifelong dream, starting my own business, and went all in.

The part nobody warned me about

Inside a company, execution is held up by scaffolding you stop noticing: deadlines with people behind them, a team waiting on your call, a calendar full of forcing functions. The irony is that I was usually the scaffolding, the one holding everyone else accountable for delivering. So when I went solo and it all disappeared overnight, I kept waiting for my own discipline to fill the gap. It never did, because knowing what to do and doing it are two entirely different skills.

I had the strategy. I had the plans; they were beautiful. And still, the boring-but-important work kept losing to whatever was more interesting that day. Time blindness would eat the afternoon. Then the guilt would kick in, and here's the cruel part: feeling bad about an avoided task pushes you further from it, not closer. Add the paralysis of choosing among twenty open loops, and days would slip by where the most important thing on the list didn't get touched. Not because I didn't care. Because there was no structure left to catch me. And no productivity app fixed it, since every one was a planning tool, and planning was never my problem.

A late ADHD diagnosis put the final piece in place: this was never a lack of discipline. My brain simply doesn't generate structure from the inside the way it can execute against structure from the outside. You can't discipline your way out of a wiring difference, but you can build for it.

So I built it

Ankora is the accountability partner I needed: the AI that gets you through the first ten minutes. Because here's what I learned about myself, and what turns out to be true for a lot of people: once I actually start, I can finish what I need to do. The whole battle is the first ten minutes. So Ankora takes the thing you're avoiding and hands back one step small enough to start right now, then stays with me the whole way: timers, check-ins, reminders, and a memory of my context so I never start from zero.

The ADHD community has been describing this exact gap, and inventing workarounds for it, for years. It turns out I'd been one of them all along, just undiagnosed. Ankora is built with those tools baked in (body doubling, tiny first steps, external structure), and it's built for anyone whose hard part is starting, diagnosis or not.

I use Ankora every day to build Ankora. That loop keeps it honest: when something in the product creates friction, I feel it before you do.

If any of this sounds like your brain, I'd genuinely love to hear from you: hello@ankorahq.com. I read everything.
